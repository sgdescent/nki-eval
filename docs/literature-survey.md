# Literature survey and research direction

Survey date: 22 October 2026. This is a focused survey of primary papers, author repositories, and AWS documentation, not an exhaustive systematic review. Dates below are the submission or revision dates displayed by arXiv, rather than search-engine crawl dates. Published measurements belong to their authors; we have not reproduced their experiments.

## Position and evidence

The strongest next direction for nki-eval is **measuring evaluator reliability and making performance claims auditable on Trainium**. Building a PyTorch-to-NKI checker is useful infrastructure, but existing work already covers NKI generation, optimization, and profiling. A claim to be the first NKI evaluator or benchmark would be unsupported. Our current contribution is a reproducible NKI-specific engineering study; research novelty remains to be established.

Our verified starting point is the 21 October run at commit `0ebffec7e76fe1e0df52dc94c7053a294b8f285d`: two example kernels, six workload cases, and both simulator and hardware integration paths passed. The hardware was `trn1.2xlarge`, with only NeuronCore 0 visible. Candidate host-request medians were 0.160–0.323 ms; speedups against compiled PyTorch on the same accelerator were 0.878–1.004×. These include transfers and host overhead. They demonstrate integration and correctness on the configured cases, not a meaningful speedup win, device-only latency, security against hostile submissions, or coverage of arbitrary kernels. The [historical summary](../validation/trainium-20261021.json) is public; underlying reports remain in the ignored local `runs/trainium-20261021/` directory. The [22 October update](pi-update-20261022.md) records the subsequent six-family validation and revised benchmark protocol.

## Findings that change the implementation plan

**NKI already has substantial prior art.** AccelOpt's NKIBench includes NumPy references, shape configurations, initial NKI kernels, and a profiling service. Its paper uses multiple random seeds and task-specific numerical tolerances, separates compilation from execution, and estimates hardware limits. The [released dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Genghan/NKIBench) documents a profiler and legacy `neuronxcc.nki` kernels. Our PyTorch interface is a useful integration choice, not enough by itself to establish a research contribution. Start with an adapter for a small compatible subset; pin source revisions and retain licensing before redistributing code. [AccelOpt §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15915v2).

NKI-Agent's body calls its generation benchmark **NKIGen-Bench**, explicitly distinguishing it from AccelOpt's **NKIBench**; its abstract uses the ambiguous name NKIBench. Its compile-and-verify loop uses PyTorch references on Trn1 and `atol=rtol=1e-3`. The paper describes a balanced 150-task evaluation subset and a separate 60-task subset. Its reported pass rates should not be compared numerically with our small set of hand-authored examples. Its binary-reward training experiment also motivates testing feedback design before investing in RL. We inspected the paper, not a downloadable reproduction package for these agent experiments. [NKI-Agent §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04395v1).

**Verifier correctness is an independent experimental subject.** MooreEval describes static checks plus runtime inspection for forbidden `aten::*` operations, and separates candidate failures from infrastructure failures. Those are architectural ideas to test, not evidence that our static import filter offers equivalent protection. [MusaCoder §4.3 and Appendix I](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04847v1). TritonRL similarly distinguishes syntax, functionality, compilation, and numerical correctness; its original formulation combines rules with an LLM judge. We should use deterministic, reproducible checks as the acceptance authority and treat any semantic model review as auxiliary evidence. [TritonRL v1 §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17891v1).

KernelBench-Verified tests scaled and negated inputs and distinguishes legitimate shape specialization from shortcuts that exploit sampled values. It also shows that baseline settings can change performance conclusions. These findings support holding the advertised shape contract fixed while adding unseen values, domain-valid boundary cases, and explicit precision settings. They do not imply that four distributions prove correctness. [KernelBench-Verified §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16241v1). The workload-reach study's zero-output counterexamples motivate testing the comparator itself with deliberately wrong outputs, especially when reference magnitudes approach absolute tolerance. A global norm metric alone can also hide localized errors; report elementwise and norm diagnostics with operation-specific acceptance criteria. [Workload reach study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21058).

**Mutation-based verifier assessment is established prior work.** Measuring the Checker scores numerical oracles against mutations with demonstrated counterexamples, filters equivalent or unwitnessed variants, checks that test inputs do not reject valid implementations through floating-point variance, and evaluates held-out faults. Its limitations explicitly restrict conclusions to the selected fault model. A small hand-chosen corpus like ours therefore supports regression evidence, not broad detection claims. The useful next step is to investigate native NKI defect families and benchmark-lifecycle failures under a documented contract, while measuring false rejections as well as missed defects. [Measuring the Checker §§3, 6, and 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22220v1). Its [released artifact](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Elfsong/KernelBench-M) contains correction records; pin a revision before reproducing or adapting its data.

**Timing scopes must remain separate.** AWS's older [`nki.benchmark` documentation](https://awsdocs-neuron.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/v2.26.1/nki/api/generated/nki.benchmark.html) describes device benchmarking and NEFF/trace artifacts. The [2.31.1 profiling guide](https://awsdocs-neuron.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/v2.31.1/nki/guides/use-neuron-profile.html) describes `neuron-explorer capture` and labels that guide for Trn2/Trn3. Therefore the installed Trn1 SDK must determine the supported capture route; copying a current tutorial is insufficient. Preserve our host-request metric and add separately labeled device metrics only after the capture path is validated. Compare candidate and baseline using the same scope and precision. The [AWS sample repository](https://github.com/aws-neuron/nki-samples) is a source of reference implementations and numerical/performance tests, but each borrowed example still needs validation on our pinned SDK.

## Three feasible research questions

### RQ1: Which checks detect realistic wrong or cheating kernels?

**Hypothesis:** varied, withheld inputs plus checks during the benchmark lifecycle catch failures missed by fixed public inputs and static lint. This applies existing mutation-assessment methodology to our NKI evaluator; it is not a claim to invent that methodology.

Build a reviewed corpus containing correct implementations and deliberately defective variants: omitted tail writes, wrong reduction axis, incorrect broadcasting, dtype loss, zero/identity shortcuts, sign-specific behavior, input mutation, stale cached answers, delayed corruption, timeouts, and checker/result tampering. Separate numerical bugs, fallback-policy violations, and attacks on process isolation. Confirm a counterexample for each numerical mutant; exclude semantically equivalent variants from the mutation denominator. A legitimate shape specialization within the declared contract is a positive control.

Compare cumulative gates: existing checks; expanded public distributions; withheld seeds and domain-valid boundaries; interleaved fresh-input validation; then protected orchestration. Measure detected/eligible mutants by category, false rejection of correct controls, stage of detection, evaluator crashes, and time added. Report exact counts and Wilson intervals, but do not treat hand-selected mutants as a random sample of all possible attacks. Freeze a second, withheld mutation set before tuning checks.

Most harness tests run locally. Run only confirmed NKI cases on the simulator and then Trn1. Subprocess separation alone is not a sandbox: arbitrary submission code currently shares the worker with the checker and can inherit host capabilities. A trusted parent must own results and reference outputs; stronger privilege separation and environment restrictions require explicit adversarial validation before claiming safe untrusted execution.

### RQ2: When do host and device measurements rank kernels differently?

**Hypothesis:** the scope of measurement changes the apparent value of optimizations, especially for small operations.

Use six families: addition, fused add/ReLU, row reduction, softmax, normalization, and matrix multiplication. Begin with three supported shapes per family, including a tile boundary or tail where the kernel contract permits it. Test FP32 first and add BF16 only after documenting accumulation and acceptance tolerances. Compare simple correct NKI, an optimized variant, and compiled PyTorch. Keep all input-generation, compilation, warmup, transfer, synchronization, and timing boundaries explicit.

On the existing `trn1.2xlarge`, reserve one visible core for measurements and serialize all benchmark jobs. Perform five independent sessions, each with 20 warmups and 200 alternating candidate/baseline measurements, retaining every sample. Capture device traces separately to avoid contaminating host timing. Record cold and cached compilation separately. Report per-case median, spread across sessions, host/device ranking agreement, and `fast_1.05` over all scheduled supported cases. Publish failures and unsupported cases separately; show conditional geometric means only with their denominator. For small datasets, show the raw session values alongside any interval. Predeclare 5% as a practical threshold, not a universal significance boundary. No speedup claim should depend on selecting the best run.

### RQ3: Does structured evaluator feedback improve repair efficiency?

**Hypothesis:** numerical counterexamples and classified failures produce more valid repairs per evaluation budget than a bare pass/fail response.

After RQ1's gates are stable, use 12–20 fixed tasks drawn from the supported families and a frozen set of initial defective candidates. Compare binary feedback, raw compiler/runtime logs, and structured diagnostics containing shape/dtype differences, numerical error location, and stage. Use one fixed generator/version, identical initial candidates, at most five repair attempts, and matched token/tool budgets. Run three generation seeds where supported. Keep final held-out values separate from the repair feedback and prevent task-specific fixes from modifying the evaluator.

Measure final held-out correctness, evaluations and wall time to first correct solution, compilation failure categories, model cost, and correctness-gated performance. Keep each complete trajectory, including failed attempts. This is an initial repair study, not evidence of general generation ability or an RL training result. The experiment can yield a useful negative result if richer feedback adds cost without improving correctness. Kevin's iteration results and CUDA Agent's larger training system motivate the question but do not establish the outcome on NKI. [Kevin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11948), [CUDA Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24286).

## Priority and evidence required for the PI update

1. **Reliability release:** mutation corpus, detection matrix, correct-control results, and a documented threat model. Acceptance: every included known numerical defect has a reproducible counterexample; unresolved tampering gaps remain visible.
2. **Coverage and measurement release:** broader operators, verified SDK support, matched host/device timing where available, and repeatable report generation. Acceptance: every plotted point links to source identity, configuration, raw results, and timing scope.
3. **Research pilot:** feedback ablation with fixed budgets and held-out evaluation. Acceptance: a reproducible comparison with failures, costs, and uncertainty retained, regardless of which condition wins.

Parallelize literature, local tests, workload authoring, and report tooling. Serialize accelerator timing and prioritize simulator failures before paid hardware runs. Defer distributed execution, large-scale RL, and broad architecture claims until these smaller experiments justify them. The most defensible near-term PI result is a measured account of what the evaluator catches, what it misses, and which timing choices change conclusions—not a promised performance record.
